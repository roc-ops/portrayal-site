Every physical thing,
individually addressable.

Portrayal turns a YAML device manifest into one flat SVG in real millimetres, where every port, PSU, fan, LED, bay and region carries a stable DOM id and a data-path. Colour a port from telemetry, light a failed PSU, link a region to a ticket, export the whole thing to a DCIM or a diagram tool.

The core is domain-neutral. Networking is the first profile, not the only one.

One source of truth. Seven ways to use it.

Six of the tabs below drive a single compiled artefact — an Edgecore AS7726-32X — six different ways. The seventh stacks six real devices into a rack and renders that same stack as a 2D elevation and as a 3D rack you can walk round.

Why it is not the other thing

The chassis view has been built five times. Always closed, always per-SKU.

CiscoView, EPN Manager, Meraki Switch Port View, Juniper Mist front panels, Arista CloudVision. And no standard anywhere — ENTITY-MIB, RFC 8348, OpenConfig, Redfish, DMTF CIM — says where a component physically is on a faceplate.

Provenance is a field, not a comment

Every dimension records where it came from — datasheet, drawing, measured, photo-measured, estimated. A device declares a maturity level and the linter holds it to that standard: verified forbids an estimated value anywhere in the assembly, including inside the components it places.

"Is this model trustworthy" is a question the tooling answers.

Modelled the way the panel is made

Punched, then printed, then populated. Chassis silkscreen paints under the components that cover it, because that is what happens to the real panel. --without silkscreen gives you the bare panel-and-components drawing to hand to whoever does the artwork.

Physical ids follow the silkscreen

What the NOS calls an interface is an overlay. Two operating systems on the same hardware disagree about naming, and the hardware does not care. Physical ids are position-based and NOS-neutral; logical names join on through entity-map rules.

No vendor material is redistributed

Facts are transcribed and cited; the source documents stay out of the repository. Datasheets are registered by title, URL and SHA-256 rather than copied. Community skins carry no vendor logos — contracts reserve a logo-zone instead.

How it works

Three layers, one build artefact.

  1. Skins

    Plain SVG in real millimetres, one per component face. Artist territory. The only obligation is to expose the contracted element ids at the contracted geometry.

  2. Contracts and manifests

    YAML, validated by JSON Schema. A component contract declares dimensions, sub-element boxes, connection points, states. A device manifest places instances of them. A NOS overlay maps names on top without ever being drawn.

  3. Compiled SVG

    Flat, components inline-expanded, stable hierarchical ids, data-* semantics, a .state-* stylesheet, and the full source definition embedded in <metadata>.

device.yaml

placements:
  - component: common/qsfp28-cage
    id: port-1
    at: [46, 10]
    group: qsfp28
    provenance:
      source: measured
      note: "cage pitch across 32 ports"

Status

Early, and saying so.

The schemas are at v0 and will change. Eleven devices are modelled across seven vendors, at varying maturity. The API is not stable and nothing here should be built against yet without expecting to follow it.

The public repository at github.com/roc-ops/Portrayal exists but is still being populated — development is happening in a private tree until the schema settles. This page is driven by a vendored snapshot of the compiled output, not a live build.
