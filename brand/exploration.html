The brief

Brand a primitive, not a product.

Portrayal compiles YAML device manifests into flat SVG in real millimeters, where every port, PSU, fan, LED, bay and region carries a stable id and a data-path. What that enables is deliberately open — documentation, monitoring overlays, DCIM integration where clicking a port follows its connection, rack layouts, install guides. The core is domain-neutral; networking is profile #1.

So the identity is closer to a font foundry or a rendering library than to a SaaS app. The mark must not depict a use case, and it must not depict a specific piece of gear. An abstract field of elements with one singled out stays true whether those elements are ports, bays, slots or cells — and whether profile #2 is networking hardware or something nobody has modeled yet.

Two constraints come straight out of the working demo, and they shape everything below. The chrome can never compete with the canvas: the device drawing is the content, rendered in real grays and metals, and status color has to be the loudest thing on screen. And the marking swatches are reserved: magenta, green, yellow, cyan and orange belong to the user's annotations. A brand color drawn from that set would read as a mark somebody made.

Anodized brass answers both. It sits nowhere near the marking palette, it never needs to appear on the canvas, and it is warm against a cool ground — a pairing that reads as panel hardware without a single port being drawn, in a category where nearly everything is blue, purple or teal.

Six candidates

One field. Six ways to single something out.

Every candidate shares the same field — four quiet elements and one tall one, irregular on purpose so it reads as a device rather than a grid. What changes is the act. Each card shows the mark at 16px and 24px, then knocked back to a single color, because those are the sizes and conditions it actually has to survive.

01Select

One element lit, wrapped in a selection ring. The most literal reading of "this one, out of all of them" — and the closest to what a user does in the explorer. Holds together at every size because the ring does the work when the fill gets too small to see.

16 · 24 · mono

02Marquee

Four corner ticks around an element that is otherwise identical to its neighbors. Says addressable rather than active — nothing is lit, something is merely reachable. Quieter and more tool-like; the ticks thin out badly below 20px.

16 · 24 · mono

03Callout

A leader line off the lit element to a label that isn't there yet. The most expressive of the six and the one that scales into a whole system — the leader can grow into real callouts across the site. Also the most fragile: a 2px diagonal is the first thing to vanish at favicon size.

16 · 24 · mono

04Lift

The element pulled out of the plane, leaving its recess behind. Nods at the 3D work and at modularity — a thing that seats into a bay and can come back out. Reads well large; at 16px the recess and the lifted copy start to merge into one blur.

16 · 24 · mono

05Aperture

Inversion: the singled-out element is the one that isn't filled. A hole in the field where everything else is solid. The strongest small-size performer here — a knockout survives anything — but it says empty bay as readily as selected, which is a real ambiguity in this domain.

16 · 24 · mono

06Trace

The lit element with a path leaving it — click a port, follow the connection. The only candidate that points outward, at integration rather than inspection. Risks reading as a network-diagram logo, which is the one neighbourhood this project should avoid.

16 · 24 · mono

Palette

One warm accent, on a ground borrowed from the app.

The grays are the demo's own, tightened and given a slight cool bias so the brass has something to be warm against. Brass appears in the mark, the site, and the chrome — never on the canvas.

Typography

A sturdy grotesque, an engineering sans, and a silkscreen voice.

Three faces, three jobs, no overlap. Deliberately not the defaults — Archivo has the squared-off density of panel lettering, and Plex carries an engineering pedigree that Inter does not.

Display Archivo 700/800. Headlines and the wordmark. Tight tracking, never below 1.4rem.
Every port, every part, individually addressable
Body IBM Plex Sans 400/500. Running copy and docs, set near 65 characters.
A device is a YAML manifest that places reusable component contracts. The compiler resolves it into one flat SVG in real millimeters, where every element carries a stable DOM id and a data-path.
Utility IBM Plex Mono 500. Ids, paths, provenance, callout labels, eyebrows. Uppercase gets 0.14em.

Still open

What I'd want decided before any of this gets drawn properly.

Which mark

My call is 01 Select for the primary, with 05 Aperture held in reserve as the favicon-and-monochrome variant if 01 proves too soft at 16px in real use.

How irregular the field

The current field is mildly irregular. It could go further — a real silhouette taken from one of the modeled devices — at the cost of the neutrality the project depends on.

Light mode for the site

The app is dark and always will be. The site could be light and lean on the drawing-sheet look, at the cost of every screenshot punching a hole in the page.

.io or .dev as canonical

One is the project, one redirects. .dev reads as the developer substrate this is; .io reads as a product. The wordmark lockup differs depending which one leads.
